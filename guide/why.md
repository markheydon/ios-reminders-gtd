[Getting Things Done](https://gettingthingsdone.com/) is a way to get commitments out of your head and into a system you trust enough to stop rehearsing them (running the same worries on a loop in your head). David Allen’s phrase for that trust is “mind like water”: the right reaction at the right moment, and a quiet head the rest of the time.

This guide is written for Reminders on iOS 27 and iPadOS 27. Reminders can be that **trusted system** on an iPhone or iPad, because capture is already with you. Siri, the share sheet, widgets, and Control Centre can land a thought in **Inbox** before you have decided what it means. On iPhone, the Action button and Lock Screen widgets are extra capture paths. The rest of this guide is how to decide, and how to see the right handful of actions when you are at home, out, or about to phone someone.

## What usually breaks

People abandon [Getting Things Done](https://gettingthingsdone.com/) for a boring reason. The weekly review gets skipped, **Inbox** fills up, and the lists stop being true. The fix is a smaller system you will still open on a tired Thursday, plus a review short enough to finish.

A second failure mode is taxonomy. A tag for every room, app, person, and energy level feels thorough on day one and becomes a filing job by day ten. This guide keeps the required tags to the constraints that change what you can do next.

## Modern contexts

Classic context lists assumed a desktop you sometimes sat at. That split is gone. If an action needs a browser, a notes app, or a bit of typing, you can almost always do it wherever you are. Calling that `@computer` hides it in a list you never open, or dumps everything into one useless pile.

The contexts that still change your behaviour:

- **Anywhere.** The default. Reading, writing, booking, paying, deciding, sending a message you can send alone.
- **Home.** Your body has to be there: a leaking tap, a form on the printer, the loft.
- **Out.** Your body has to be somewhere else: the post office, a shop, a specific building. A location alert can nudge you when you arrive. The tag is what puts it on the list you glance at before you leave.
- **Calls.** Another person has to be live with you. Messages you can send alone stay in **Anywhere**. “Next time I am already chatting to them” is the When Messaging switch, described in [projects](projects.md).

Energy, time available, and deep-focus work are real, and they are optional. They live in [advanced](advanced.md) so the everyday system stays at one tag per action.

## A system you will keep

Trust comes from two promises you can keep:

- Every commitment has a home, and **Inbox** gets cleared.
- When you open **Home**, **Out**, **Calls**, or **Anywhere**, you see actions you could do in that situation, sitting under the project they belong to.

The weekly review is the practice. The tags are just how the phone or tablet groups the work.

If you are starting again after a break, the restart path lives in the opening of [setup](setup.md).
